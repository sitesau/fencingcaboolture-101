The first sign of trouble is not always a high price. It is often a rushed brief. If a provider talks about Colorbond, timber, PVC or rural wire before asking where the line sits, what is already standing, how the block is accessed and whether the fence is shared, the quote may be guessing. A useful early read is whether the conversation mirrors the planning logic in Fencing Contractors Caboolture: 2026 Guide: boundary first, materials second.

  • No questions about narrow side access, slopes, pets, trees, retaining walls or gates.
  • No separation between a boundary replacement, pool barrier, driveway gate, acreage run or landscaping-linked job.
  • Preferences treated as fixed rules, or compliance needs treated as optional styling.

Quote red flags that matter

A weak quote hides assumptions. Ask for the run to be measured, the ground and access inspected, and the existing fence material identified before removal is priced separately from installation. For a shared boundary, the quote should be itemised enough to hand to the neighbour without re-explaining the whole job.

  • Deposit requested at enquiry stage, before scope is settled.
  • One total figure with no line items for removal, gates, posts, height changes or pool work.
  • Pool fencing described as ordinary privacy fencing, even though glass and aluminium pool barriers must meet the Queensland pool safety standard and be signed off by a licensed pool safety inspector.
  • Gate hardware chosen without reference to the fence line, despite sliding, swing and solar-powered automation needing to match the opening, posts and style.

Boundary, height and neighbour signals

A fence on the actual boundary is a dividing fence. Under the Neighbourhood Disputes (Dividing Fences and Trees) Act 2011, adjoining owners are generally responsible equally up to a sufficient dividing fence, broadly 0.5 m to 1.8 m high and able to be built from prescribed materials such as palings, chain wire, metal panels or brick.

The warning sign is blurred responsibility. If one household wants a taller run, premium finish or Colorbond where a sufficient fence would do, that upgrade belongs on the requesting owner's bill. A formal notice to contribute should set out the land, fence line, work and estimated cost, with a written quote attached. Allow one month for agreement, then two further months for either owner to take the matter to QCAT if no deal is reached.

Site conditions that change the answer

Material choice should follow conditions rather than habit. Colorbond is a common boundary option; timber paling, lapped-and-capped and picket styles suit character streets and family blocks; PVC is a repaint-free option with salt air off Moreton Bay relevant near the coast; acreage blocks move toward strained wire and star pickets.

Watch for three compliance traps. A fence on top of a retaining wall counts the wall in the measurement, so height can change quickly. Corner blocks can raise sight-line limits for front fences. Around 2 m, a fence generally needs certifier sign-off, so a casual promise to build higher without explaining approval is a warning sign.

  1. Define the job. Write whether it is a boundary replacement, pool barrier, driveway gate, acreage fence, retaining-wall-adjacent run or mixed fencing and landscaping task.
  2. Record constraints. Note boundary status, existing material and height, access limits, pets, trees, retaining walls, corner sight lines and pool compliance needs.
  3. Request an itemised quote. Ask for measurement, ground and access inspection, separate removal and installation lines, and clear treatment of gates and pool work.
  4. Confirm compliance and sharing. Check regulated work licensing, certifier questions near 2 m, and dividing fence cost sharing before any notice to contribute is sent.
